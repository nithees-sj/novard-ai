Today support is reactive: a problem happens, someone complains, it gets discovered and investigated, and only then does anyone act. NOVARD turns that around: signals lead to a prediction, the prediction is verified, and action happens early, before the first complaint. The pipeline below shows how. Signals flow through feature engineering into a trained XGBoost model that predicts risk. Gemini then acts as an independent validation and explanation layer around that prediction. It adds context and does not prove the model's output. The admin always makes the final decision.

Flow A is any student request. The React app calls the Express API, which handles authentication and rate limiting. The request goes either to a feature service, when the student creates something themselves, or to the Novard Agent, when they delegate it. Both load context from MongoDB and Vector Search, generate with Groq-hosted GPT-OSS through LangChain, then save and return the result. Flow B runs alongside: every request leaves signals and logs, which feed the risk pipeline from feature engineering to XGBoost, Gemini and the admin dashboard.

This is the full AI pipeline behind risk prediction. Offline, historical signals and their outcomes are labelled, turned into features, used to train XGBoost, evaluated, and saved as a versioned model. Live, new signals are enriched with sentiment and urgency from the text, engineered into the same features, and scored by the deployed model into a risk probability and level. Gemini then validates and explains the prediction, and the admin decides on the intervention. Outcomes become new labels and the model is retrained, so the system improves over time.
